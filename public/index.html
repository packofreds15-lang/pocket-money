        Cheat sheet

        The ideas worth remembering if you forget everything else.

        Money

        1. Know where your money goes.Writing down what you spend for just one month usually shows you something surprising.
        2. Pay yourself first.Put some money aside for saving as soon as you get it, before you start spending.
        3. Wait a day before buying wants.If you still want it tomorrow, it's probably worth it. Many impulse buys don't survive the wait.
        4. Never share your OTP or UPI PIN.You never need a PIN to receive money. Anyone asking for one is trying to take it.
        5. Borrowing costs money.Interest means you pay back more than you borrowed, so avoid borrowing for things you only want.

        Investing

        1. A share is a small piece of a real business.When the business grows and earns more, the share usually becomes worth more over time.
        2. Only invest money you won't need for at least five years.Short‑term money belongs in savings, where it can't drop right before you need it.
        3. Higher possible returns come with higher risk.Anyone promising big, guaranteed returns is either wrong or running a scam.
        4. Don't put all your eggs in one basket.Owning many companies across different industries means one bad result can't sink you.
        5. Index funds are the simplest way to diversify.One purchase gives you a small slice of hundreds of companies, usually with low fees.
        6. Prices go up and down; that's normal.Selling in a panic after a drop turns a temporary dip into a permanent loss.
        7. Time is your biggest advantage.Compound growth needs years to work, and teenagers have more years than anyone.
        8. Fees quietly eat your returns.A 1–2% yearly fee sounds tiny but can cost a large share of your final amount over decades.
        9. Be skeptical of social media tips.Ask who benefits if you buy, check reliable sources, and never invest in something you can't explain.
        10. Talk to a trusted adult first.In India and most countries you need a parent or guardian to open an investment account until you're 18.

        The problem

        Teenagers see investing all over social media, but few understand the basics behind stocks, risk or saving, and most money advice isn't written for them.
